A knitting story

Mara and the first swatch

Follow one strand of yarn through a knitting machine and learn the words that describe its journey.

01

The loom room

Mara enters a workshop carrying a cone of yarn. In front of her is a V-bed knitting machine: two long rows of metal needles face each other like two sides of a narrow valley. They are called the front bed and the back bed.

Each needle can hold one or more loops. A needle position is therefore both a place on the machine and a place where the fabric can grow.

02

The traveler and the guide

Mara threads the yarn through a carrier, the small guide that brings yarn to the needles. A carriage travels across the beds. One trip from one side to the other is a pass.

The carriage has a direction—left or right—and the order in which it meets the needles follows that direction.

03

A loop becomes fabric

At the first selected needle, the yarn bends into a loop. A stitch is the action and structure created when a new loop is drawn through an older one. The older loop supports the new loop; many such relationships form the fabric.

A horizontal sequence of stitches made during a pass is a course, often called a row in everyday knitting. Vertical chains of related loops are called wales.

04

Three choices at every needle

As the carrier reaches a needle, the machine can knit, drawing a new loop through the held loop; tuck, adding a new loop while the old one remains; or miss, carrying the yarn past without forming a loop there.

A loop can also be transferred from one bed to the other. Transfers change where loops are held and make richer structures possible.

05

From loose yarn to a swatch

The opening operations that place yarn securely on empty needles are the cast-on. Once the yarn is established, repeated courses grow downward from the beds. A small sample made to test this fabric is a swatch.

Stockinette is a smooth basic fabric formed by knit stitches arranged in courses and wales. Its two faces look different even though they belong to the same fabric.

The machine's diary

Knitout records this journey as a sequence of machine operations: which action happened, in which direction, on which needle, and with which carrier. Reading it is like following the machine's diary one pass at a time.
